# Changelog

Notable changes, newest first. Dates are when the work was merged or finished.

## Unreleased
- Series 65 added to `docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`: a payday-Saturday correction instead of a payday feature, on the saved predictions of the deployed pieces (nothing is refitted). The existing Saturday correction lifts every Saturday by about £230 although payday Saturdays still miss by £701 and others by −£93, so two rules were tried, from earlier folds only: a split correction (payday Saturdays and other Saturdays corrected separately) and a stacked offset. Both gain about £2 a day in the backtest (intervals −£7 to +£4 and −£9 to +£4, a gain in about 75% of resamples, helped on 10 or 11 of the 17 days changed) but do worse on the 56 new days, where two of the three payday Saturdays were exceptional (24 October, named storms and 24 mm of rain; 31 October, Halloween). Not adopted; 28 November 2026 is the next payday Saturday. New script `run_series65_payday_saturday_correction.py`. No model, feature list or pipeline code changed.
- Series 64 added to `docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`: the parts of the original payday plan that were not yet run: windows of 1, 2 and 5 days around the last-working-day payday, a graded signed distance (each added as one column), and the 25th, 28th and 15th rebuilt as full column sets replacing the four deployed payday columns. Nothing helps: the windows and distance are −£1.0 to +£2.5, every interval includes zero (the signed distance is better on the 56 new days but not in the backtest); the date rules are £4.5 to £6.8 worse than the deployed columns and indistinguishable from the last working day when compared with Series 59's repair (within £2, intervals about ±£10). The payday-feature line is closed. New script `run_series64_payday_plan_rest.py`. No model, feature list or pipeline code changed.
- Series 62 and 63 added to `docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`: why do the deployed payday columns beat their repairs? The deployed `days_since_payday` is the day of the month plus an offset of 0 to 3 days on all 1,006 days (correlation 0.995). Series 62 replaces it with plain `day_of_month` (+£3.0 a day, 1 of 10 folds better), keeps the ramp with the other two columns repaired (+£2.6; the reset explains about half of Series 59's loss) and rebuilds a true ramp with true distances (+£6.2); Series 63 splits the offset into its own column (+£6.8 replacing, +£2.9 adding, intervals about ±£7 to ±£10). The explanation is not found. New scripts `run_series62_payday_ramp.py` and `run_series63_payday_gap.py`. No model, feature list or pipeline code changed.
- Series 59 to 61 added to `docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`: the payday family in the weekly log's pieces, 100 seeds. Series 59 repairs the flawed payday columns (`days_since_payday` counts from the previous month's payday; `days_to_payday` goes negative on the days after an early payday): the repair is £3 to £5 a day worse than the deployed columns, and dropping the four payday columns is £5 to £8 worse. Series 60 adds explicit "weekend after an early payday" flags: none helps (+£0.9 to +£3.7), and the flag does not recover what the repair lost. Series 61, built from the deployed model's own errors (selection bias stated beforehand), adds payday dates and windows for the last working day, 25th, 28th and 15th: nothing helps, and the best backtest result (a Friday-payday weekend flag, −£2.4) is £11.8 worse on the 56 new days. The deployed columns stay. New scripts `run_series60_payday_weekend.py` and `run_series61_payday_rules.py` (Series 59's script was committed earlier). The README's Future Work records the result and the ideas still open. No model, feature list or pipeline code changed.

The final model gets steadily worse as the weighting sharpens (45 days: +£158; 90 days: +£31; 730 days, barely a weighting: +£1.5), because the effective sample size falls from about 635 rows to as few as 126; the least bad fails its bar on fresh seeds (Friday +£20, significantly worse). - Series 33 added to `docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`: the first-week correction forward-chained on the leave-one-break-out forecasts of all six Easter and summer breaks, pooled mean against per-type against pooled median. The pooled-median rule is pre-registered for the Easter and summer 2027 breaks. New script `run_series33_first_week_by_type.py` and tests.
- Series 32 added to `docs/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` (accuracy roadmap, Track B): a correction for the first week of the Easter and summer breaks.
